## Blog draft — owner review required; unpublished

**Proposed title: Building UMBRAL with Kiro: from CSE preparation to a clearer path toward opportunities**

This post is being prepared as part of the **Kiroween 2026 hackathon**. UMBRAL helps people understand an opportunity, inspect its requirements and prepare their next steps. It began with a narrower problem: explaining the first household socioeconomic classification (CSE) request in Chiclayo, Peru, without pretending to decide eligibility or submit a government application.

Kiro created the original React/TypeScript/Vite implementation and left three Specs and five Steering files. The requirements defined the audience, preparation guide, locally saved checklist, official references, Spanish/English content and offline reading after an initial load. The design described the architecture; tasks divided the implementation into phases. Steering documented product boundaries, architecture, quality, security and UX expectations.

The useful part of this evidence is its specificity. A checklist is local preparation, not an official approval. A source link lets someone check the institution's information, but does not guarantee a benefit. Those distinctions are visible in the original planning documents, whose eight files remain unchanged from the initial commit.

Later Codex work audited failures, corrected defects, strengthened tests and deployment, introduced The Illuminated Threshold visual direction and expanded the opportunity experience. That work is credited to Codex. I am not presenting it as Kiro output, and I am not claiming Agent Hooks, MCP, Crew or property-based testing without evidence.
